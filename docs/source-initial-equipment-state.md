# Source initial equipment and current equipment state

The source can assign an initial six-slot loadout which the player would not be allowed to select from the equipment menu. `AddFromMaster` reads the actual `character_status.content_id1..6` rows and calls `AddOwnedItem` and `SetEquip` in order. Its `FixedEquip` owner checks slots, dominant hand, equipment effects and registration; it does not call the separate job eligibility predicate. The menu's `CanEquipped` reads `job_group.GetJobAccept(jobId) == 1` before an incoming selection.

`source-equipment-state-v2` records this distinction in the trusted equipment catalog. Existing current assignments retain every executable effect, source identity, Parts/hand, provider, inventory and owner check. Job acceptance constrains a player's incoming choice. A job-ineligible initial piece remains equipped after initialization, source rebind, parameter projection, battle borrowing and persistence. Once removed, it cannot be selected again through the player menu. Runtime code does not identify a character, scene or script PC to grant this behavior.

`equipment-development-v1` retains its prior shape and existing-state job check. Both kinds have the same definitions and character fields; a new kind selects a different contract. `compileEquipmentCatalog` has an explicit final kind argument, defaulting to v1. `compileOwnedCharacterInitialization` and `compileNewGame` can receive that contract explicitly, while source battle compilation and the current shared game-state catalog select v2. The original three-person source new-game definition remains byte-identical with its v1 initial equipment catalog; potential later templates do not become new-game owners. The player's incoming check remains strict for both catalog kinds. No kind or policy is accepted from a save.

GameState continues to own the only roster, total equipment stock, three Corps groups, job history, learned history, names and character resources. Dynamic initialization returns a detached candidate. Growth, parameter projection and real Field actor preparation must succeed before publication. A failed candidate cannot add an owner or equipment copy; battle preparation and return keep the existing lease and final guard boundaries. The module does not add a second equipment owner or new save fields.

## Complete source scope

The nomination retains all 3304 programs / 186003 instructions, 649 maps and 34 character master records. Its shared party workspace contains 595 calls in 235 complete programs / 31249 operations and 97 map closures / 1633 assets: 144 roster calls, 220 Corps organization/selection/query instructions, 52 party-selection calls, 121 Puppet replacements and 58 RTS calls. Another 550 owned condition/recovery operations remain contextual data. All 53 battle programs / 2312 operations and 490 party requests (483 descriptors plus seven diagnostics) remain intact.

The fixed source's complete initialization/growth templates increase from 18 to 27. The newly recovered templates are source 10, 20–25, 27 and 28. These numbers describe the supplied data, not runtime exceptions. Their original six items, slot flags, job groups and parameters are unchanged. The other 25 source records preserve their previous complete template or explicit blocker. Source 4/14/33 still require initial condition owners; 9 requires its resistance equipment effect; 16 requires its MP-cost equipment effect; 30/34 lack a unique initialization experience curve.

The 595 instruction lowerings do not change: 506 typed calls and 89 explicit stops remain. In particular, the 17 special Gau/Ghost/Moogles sites, 12 NPC dictionary sites, 58 RTS sites and two final-party ordering calls do not gain authority. Providing all initial equipment data does not recover the grouped Moogles creation/removal order. No character is injected into held state, and no flag or initial party is fabricated.

The current battle export contains media for the old 18 identities. The nine new templates therefore still encounter a specific missing battle media/definition gate. Ordinary battle definitions remain 18 until a separately verified media snapshot is integrated. Source state-owner probes must report that rejection; Field frames cannot stand in for `Idle_00`. Template coverage, actual Session mechanics, full original story execution and original executable parity are separate lanes.

## Verification

`node scripts/check-source-initial-equipment-state.mjs --compiled-dir <dist> --before-compiled-dir <baseline-dist> --candidate-dir <immutable-private-candidate> --output build/reports/source-initial-equipment-state.json` loads the whole source once. It compares all 34 original records, preserves all 595 instruction diagnostics and complete callers, verifies source/physical identities and retains battle/AI dependencies. Its optional candidate probes use the exact published payload binding, actual source initialization/growth and GameState transactions. Those probes are explicitly state-owner checks, without a Field presentation callback or source-story reachability claim.

`tests/equipment-source-state.test.ts` exercises legacy compatibility, source initialization, incoming job rejection and hard effect/slot/hand/stock/identity failures. `tests/equipment-source-session.test.ts` uses a declared synthetic source initializer, real native Field factory and actual GameSession. A later owner must be created by the source roster command. Tests cover publication failure, restore/retry, real actor refresh and organization, battle lease cancellation/stale candidates/unique return, Storage and continued menu rejection. They do not assert source-story or real-game media acceptance.

`node scripts/check-initial-equipment-state-native.mjs` checks six cached complete methods / 10624 original PE bytes plus DLL, symbols, header and C digests. It never starts Ghidra or runs the original game. The static receipt remains `semanticsVerified=false`.
